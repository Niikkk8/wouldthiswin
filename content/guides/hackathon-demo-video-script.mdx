A two-minute demo video should spend about 15 seconds on the problem, 75 showing the product working, 20 on how it's built, and 10 on what's next. Show the product running as early as you can; judges decide in the first 30 seconds whether to keep watching closely.

## What goes in the first 15 seconds?

One person, one problem, one sentence. "Every Saturday, the front desk at a small clinic answers the same question a hundred times: how long is the wait?" Then the name of your project and what it does in one line. No logo animation, no team intro: those come last, if at all.

## How do you show the product in 75 seconds?

Show one user getting from the problem to the result, start to finish, in the real app. Narrate what the user does, not what the code does: "She scans the QR code, joins the queue, and gets a text when she's two places away."

Cut every pause. Record the screen at a size people can read, zoom in on the moment that matters, and don't show a login screen unless logging in is the feature. If any part uses sample data, say so in the narration.

## How much of the stack should you show?

Twenty seconds, with one diagram or one screen of code at most. Name the parts a judge would ask about: where the data comes from, what the AI part does, what's deployed where. The [tech stack guide](/guides/how-to-explain-your-tech-stack-to-judges) has a structure for saying it in a few sentences.

## How do you end?

Ten seconds: what you'd build next, and the link. Put the deployed URL and the repo on screen at the end, long enough to read.

## What are the common mistakes?

- Spending 40 seconds on slides before the product appears.
- Background music louder than the narration.
- Showing a feature the README promises but the code doesn't have.
- A link at the end that's private or broken.

## Can you check the video link before you submit?

Open it in a private window. [Would This Win?](/) checks video and demo links in your README too, and flags ones that don't load. See the [submission checklist](/guides/hackathon-submission-checklist) for the rest.
