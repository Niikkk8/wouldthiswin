Hackathon judges mostly ask the same 25 or so questions, and they fall into three groups: how it's built, who it's for, and what it is. A strong answer names something specific (a file, a user, a number) and admits what isn't done yet.

## What do technical judges ask?

Technical judges test whether you built what you claim.

1. **Which file does the main feature?** Name the file and the function. Have it open.
2. **What did you build, and what did you wire together?** Say which parts are yours and which are libraries or APIs. Blurring this reads as hiding.
3. **What breaks first under real load?** Name a real weakness. "Nothing" is never the right answer.
4. **Is that real data or sample data?** If it's sample data, say so and say what the real source would be.
5. **Why this stack?** One reason per choice, tied to the problem, not to what's popular.
6. **How does the AI part work?** Which model, called from where, with what input. If it's one API call, say that plainly.
7. **What would you do with another week?** One concrete next step, not a roadmap.
8. **How do you handle errors or bad input?** One example from your code.
9. **Is it deployed, and where?** The URL, and what runs where.

## What do business judges ask?

Business judges test whether anyone needs it.

10. **Who is this for, specifically?** A named role, not a category: "the front desk at a small clinic", not "healthcare".
11. **What do they do today instead?** The workaround your project replaces.
12. **Who pays?** The person or budget, and roughly how much.
13. **Why now?** What changed that makes this possible or needed.
14. **How would you reach your first ten users?** One channel you could actually use.
15. **What's the biggest risk?** Say it before they do.
16. **Have you talked to a user?** If yes, what they said. If no, say no.
17. **How is it different from X?** One sentence, without attacking X.

## What does the tired judge ask?

The last judge of the day wants to remember you.

18. **In one sentence, what is it?** What it does and for whom, in one breath.
19. **Can you show me?** Have the demo open on the right screen.
20. **What's the one thing to remember?** Your strongest result.
21. **Why should this win?** One reason, tied to the event's criteria.
22. **What was the hardest part?** A real problem you solved, briefly.
23. **Who did what?** A clear split of the work.
24. **Is the code public?** The repo link, ready.
25. **Anything else?** Say no, or one thing you haven't covered. Don't repeat the pitch.
